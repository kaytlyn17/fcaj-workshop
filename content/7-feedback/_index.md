## Overall feedback on the program

### 1. Learning environment and working format

What I enjoyed about FCAJ was being able to start with a small question and answer it through a lab. In an early permissions exercise the question was simply which identity could open a resource. Later, when I used the CLI and deployed templates, I recognized that same question at almost every step. Independent practice gave me time to repeat a difficult exercise. In weeks with several labs, a short list of priorities would have helped me decide what to study in depth first.

### 2. Support from mentors and organizers

The sharing sessions let me hear practitioners explain why they chose a particular design. That reasoning is difficult to get from a sequence of instructions alone. I found it easier to frame a question once I could state my goal, what I had tried and what I actually observed. A shared collection of recurring questions about regions, identities and permissions would give beginners a useful first check and leave more discussion time for difficult cases.

### 3. Relevance of the curriculum

As an Information Assurance student, I found the progression from identity and networking to storage, monitoring and security especially useful. Knowing how to find a log made the incident-response material easier to follow. Migration, hybrid environments and containers also exposed me to more than one way of moving an application to the cloud. I would welcome a short exercise at each transition that calls on a skill from the previous topic.

### 4. Opportunities to develop technical skills

The biggest lesson for me was how much there is to do after a service first responds. Infrastructure as code gave me a way to inspect a proposed change. Monitoring required me to select a meaningful metric. Cost analysis required me to define the billing period and workload before interpreting a number. Sessions on AI and retrieval also gave me a new question to ask of an application: which source document supports its answer?

After the Docker, ECS and EKS labs, I would have welcomed a discussion of the same application running on containers or Lambda. Asking each learner to choose a deployment and defend it in terms of traffic, updates and running costs would bring the two topics together.

### 5. Teamwork and reporting

Keeping a weekly worklog exposed entries where I had recorded clicks without explaining the result. Writing bilingual blog posts made me consider someone who had never run the lab. I would enjoy a workshop exchange where one student follows another’s instructions in a fresh environment while the author observes. Seeing where the reader stops would reveal assumptions that are almost invisible to the person who wrote the guide.

### 6. Participation requirements and practical arrangements

For a student managing an AWS account, knowing what may continue to incur charges after a session is important. Clear prerequisites make a lab much easier to plan. A short note at the beginning listing the target region, permissions, indicative cost and resources needed for the following session would make that planning easier still.

## Further questions

**What was most satisfying?** I could see the same skill develop over time. Early on I read logs to fix a lab. Later I used logs and execution times to explain to another reader why a request reached a particular outcome. That change made my progress visible.

**What would help future participants?** I would add a short self-check at the end of each stage, with a sample input, one common failure and an expected result. A learner could then decide whether to move on or revisit a particular topic.

**Would I recommend FCAJ to a friend?** Yes, especially to someone curious about the day-to-day work of a cloud engineer. I would suggest setting aside time for notes and cleanup within each session. Both are easy to postpone as soon as the lab starts working.

## Suggestions and hopes for the future

- **For the program.** I would set aside one short peer design review. Each student would explain a decision, describe a failure the design anticipates and take a question from a peer.
- **Continuing involvement.** I hope to join more sessions on cloud security, multi-account governance and incident investigation. I would also like to write a short lab that a future participant can reproduce in their own account.
- **A closing note.** I appreciate the opportunity FCAJ gives students to try the actual work involved in running cloud systems. The habit I will take with me is keeping enough evidence to explain a technical decision to someone else.
