**Role:** Attendee

## Working backwards from the judging criteria

The organisers opened the kickoff by explaining the challenge and what teams would need to demonstrate. That gave the icebreaker a purpose. As we talked about our AWS experience and the AI problems we wanted to try, it became easier to see which skills a team could put to use and what it could reasonably deliver in a demo.

## Trying the AI agent challenge

The mentor-supported exercise brought together three ideas I had mostly encountered separately. A prompt describes the task, a knowledge base supplies reference material, and retrieval-augmented generation brings relevant passages into the model’s context. It gave me a specific question to ask when an answer seems poorly supported: which passage did the application actually retrieve? Reading that passage alongside the answer helps narrow down where to make a change.

The career Q&A followed naturally from the exercise. Questions about AWS certifications and practice projects felt more concrete after spending time on a problem with other participants and mentors.

## What I would use again

At the start of my next team challenge, I would write down one example input and the result we want to show. The team can return to that same example when it presents the demo. For PhishGuard, I would open a suspicious-message report and show the passage from a playbook that helps an analyst interpret the result.
