**Venue:** Bitexco Financial Tower, Ho Chi Minh City  
**Role:** Attendee

## Thinking about the person who will run the system

The conversation with Dr. Werner Vogels made me reconsider when I call a lab finished. When I build something on my own, my usual stopping point is the first successful run. Listening to a discussion of systems operated at a global scale made me think about the person who takes over the next day. If one processing step fails, will that person have enough information to find out what happened?

The conversation touched on cloud computing, AI and technical leadership. What stayed with me was the team consequence of an architecture decision. The builder needs to explain why a technology was chosen, and the operator needs to understand how the application behaves when something goes wrong.

## What the Q&A added

The room brought together students, developers, solution architects, operations engineers, data engineers and AI practitioners. I enjoyed hearing how people in different roles approached the same subject. It also made me want to take a more active part in discussions at future community events.

I can apply that lesson to my own workshop in a small way. Alongside a successful run, I want to point readers to the execution history they can open if a report stops progressing. That gives the next person running the lab a practical place to start.
